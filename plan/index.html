FAQ

How is the plan built?

It starts at your current weekly distance and builds gradually to a peak suited to your race and number of running days, with an easier week every fourth week. The first block is base running with hills and fartlek, then come race-specific intervals, tempo runs and longer long runs, and the last one to three weeks are a taper so you arrive fresh.

Where do the target paces come from?

From your goal time, using the Riegel formula to estimate your equivalent 5K, 10K, half and marathon performance. Easy and long runs are deliberately slow. If the workouts feel far too hard, your goal is probably ambitious: try a slower goal time and rebuild the plan.

How many weeks do I need?

Typically 8 weeks for a 5K, 10 for a 10K, 12 for a half marathon and 16 to 20 for a marathon, assuming you can already run 30 minutes comfortably. If your race is further away, the plan starts later and tells you what to do until then. If it is very close, the plan is compressed and warns you.

What do I = interval, T = threshold and so on mean?

Easy: conversational. T (threshold): comfortably hard, roughly the pace you could race for an hour. I (interval): hard, around 3K–5K race effort. R (repetition): fast and relaxed, with full recovery. HMP / MP: half-marathon and marathon goal pace. Every hard session includes about 2 km easy warm-up and cool-down.

Is this medical advice?

No. Increase training gradually, listen to your body and see a doctor if you have health concerns or pain that does not go away.
